#### Target question: keeping children engaged, safe and on schedule

My approach would be to make the day feel fun and relaxed for the children while having clear structure behind the scenes.

At the beginning I would set simple expectations around staying with the group, listening at transition points and following safety instructions. I would use regular headcounts, clear meeting points and position myself so I can maintain good awareness of the whole group. I would also work closely with the other staff so everyone knows where the group is going and whether any child needs additional support.

My Bachelor of Outdoor Education included extensive practical experience in group leadership, environmental interpretation and safety in outdoor environments. It taught me that engagement and safety work together: children are much easier to supervise when they are interested in what is happening.

I would keep activities interactive by asking questions, encouraging children to make observations, using short stories or challenges, and adapting explanations to their age and energy level rather than talking at them for long periods. At the zoo I would use the animals and surroundings to help make Taronga's conservation messages memorable and relevant.

To stay on schedule, I would know the day's plan beforehand, give children clear warnings before transitions and keep an eye on time without making the day feel rushed. If something unexpected occurred, I would communicate with the team, adapt the activity and always prioritise the safety and wellbeing of the children over simply keeping to the timetable.

My experience in schools, youth leadership and customer-facing roles has also taught me how important calm communication, encouragement and clear boundaries are when working with young people.

#### Target question: safety example

Recently at Avance Business Technology, I reviewed our workplace first-aid arrangements after completing first-aid training. I identified that some of the arrangements relied too heavily on people simply knowing what to do, rather than having everything clearly documented and routinely checked.

I reviewed the workplace requirements, confirmed the location of the first-aid kit, looked at what procedures and information were already in place and identified areas that needed to be clearer. I prepared concise first-aid and emergency documentation and took responsibility for carrying out regular checks of the first-aid kit.

My aim was to make sure that, if an incident occurred, staff could quickly identify where the equipment was, who could assist and what process should be followed rather than trying to work those things out during an emergency.

The experience reinforced something I also learnt through Outdoor Education: good safety management is proactive. It means noticing weaknesses before somebody gets hurt, checking requirements rather than relying on assumptions, communicating clearly and following through with practical action.

That is the same approach I would bring to supervising children at Taronga — remain observant, follow established procedures, raise concerns early and never let familiarity or time pressure override safety.

### Reality check

This is a stretch application at present. Josh should not present himself as an experienced keeper.

Taronga's public zookeeper pathway advises:

1. Gain hands-on animal experience.
2. Complete **ACM20121 Certificate II in Animal Care**.
3. Progress to **ACM30321 Certificate III in Wildlife and Exhibited Animal Care**.
4. Build zoo/animal-care practical experience and public-speaking/presentation skills.

Taronga's Certificate III is specifically aimed at aspiring zookeepers and wildlife carers. Entry normally builds on Certificate II or equivalent animal-related study/experience.

Useful links:

- Become a zookeeper: https://www.taronga.org.au/get-involved/work-at-taronga/become-a-zookeeper
- Certificate courses: https://www.taronga.org.au/learn/tertiary/certificate-courses
- Certificate II online: https://www.taronga.org.au/learn/tertiary/certificate-courses/certificate-ii-online
- Certificate III: https://www.taronga.org.au/learn/tertiary/certificate-courses/certificate-iii

### Keeper target-question principles

Direct experience with dangerous animals and formal animal training is limited. Horse-therapy exposure gives some experience being calm and observant around large animals, but it should not be described as professional animal handling.

Principles to emphasise:

- positive reinforcement
- consistency and clear cues
- careful observation of body language and changes from normal behaviour
- meaningful choice/agency where appropriate
- adapting to individual temperament, history, physical condition and social context
- learning established husbandry procedures before attempting unfamiliar tasks
- strict respect for barriers, procedures, communication and keeper judgement
- dynamic risk assessment and no complacency around familiar activities

### Important distinction

Some Taronga education delivery is guided by NSW Department of Education teachers. A Bachelor of Outdoor Education should not automatically be assumed to satisfy NSW teacher-accreditation requirements. Taronga-employed guest/community/education roles and Department-employed teacher roles are separate pathways.
